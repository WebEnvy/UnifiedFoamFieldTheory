## The New Model

What this case study demonstrates is not a better way to do existing research. It is a different model for how discovery works.

The old model: a physicist spends years developing a framework alone, acquires institutional support, submits to peer review, waits months, revises, resubmits. Optimised for a world without AI. Well-suited to incremental advances within established paradigms. Poor at paradigm-breaking work, which requires crossing domain boundaries and making connections that no single expert's training equips them to see.

The new model: a person with genuine insight — not necessarily institutional credentials, not necessarily mathematical training — and the ability to translate that insight into precise questions, combined with an AI capable of executing the mathematics, verifying the results, holding the entire framework in memory, and producing the documentation. The human provides vision. The AI provides execution. Neither alone produces the result on this timescale.

This model has specific requirements on both sides.

**The human must:** have genuine original insight (not just curiosity), understand the ideas well enough to ask precise questions, be able to recognise when an answer is wrong or incomplete, have the courage to publish and stand behind the work, and be willing to be honestly assessed — including being told when something isn't proven.

**The AI must:** be capable enough to do rigorous mathematics across domains, be honest about what is derived versus what is consistent versus what is speculative, catch its own errors, hold large complex frameworks in context across sessions, and produce clear publishable documentation.

Claude was the first AI capable enough for this specific task. That is stated as a factual observation, not a promotional claim. Luke Martin tried other AIs. They were not capable of what was needed. The framework was waiting for an AI that could keep up with the questions.
