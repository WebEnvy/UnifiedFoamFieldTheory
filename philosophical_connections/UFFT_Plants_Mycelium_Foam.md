## The Mycelial Network — The Most Interesting Case

### Physical facts

The largest known organism is *Armillaria ostoyae* in the Malheur National Forest, Oregon:
- Size: 9.65 km² (2,385 acres)
- Age: estimated 8,650 years old
- Mass: ~600 tonnes
- It is a single organism — genetically identical throughout

Signal propagation through hyphal networks:
- Electrical impulses: 0.5–10 mm/s
- Chemical signals: slower, but richer in information content
- Action potential-like pulses have been measured in response to environmental stimuli

The network is not static. It:
- Routes nutrients around obstacles (problem-solving)
- Strengthens heavily-used connections (Hebbian learning in biological form)
- Prunes unused connections (resource optimisation)
- Responds differently to the same stimulus in different contexts

*Physarum polycephalum* (slime mould — not technically a fungus but behaviourally analogous) solved the Tokyo rail network optimisation problem. Given food at locations corresponding to major Tokyo stations, it grew a network that closely matched the actual rail system — optimising for resilience and efficiency simultaneously. No neurons. No brain. Pure distributed computation.

### In UFFT terms

A mycelial network is a distributed topological defect that:
1. Integrates over a spatial scale of kilometres
2. Stores memory in its physical topology — the wiring IS the memory
3. Processes information through electrical and chemical D-mode exchanges
4. Optimises its own structure in response to the foam environment
5. Persists for millennia

This is not metaphor. The signal propagation in a mycelial network is electrochemical — it involves the same ion channels, the same membrane potentials, the same physical processes as neural signal propagation. The difference is architecture, not substrate.

## Plants — The Overlooked Intelligence

Recent peer-reviewed science has substantially revised our understanding of plant cognition:

**Mimosa pudica** (*sensitive plant*): learns to ignore repeated harmless drops within 60 repetitions, retaining this learned behaviour for months. This is classical habituation — the most fundamental form of learning — achieved without a nervous system.

**Root sound sensitivity**: plants route roots toward sound frequencies matching water flow, even in the complete absence of moisture. They are responding to acoustic information — D-mode pressure waves in the foam — without any sensory organ we would recognise as such.

**Kin recognition**: trees in mycorrhizal networks preferentially route carbon to their own genetic offspring. They distinguish kin from non-kin. This requires chemical discrimination and directed resource allocation — purposeful behaviour.

**Strategic chemical signalling**: plants damaged by herbivores release specific volatile chemicals that attract the predators of those herbivores — not the herbivores' predators in general, but specifically those that prey on the species currently attacking. This is multi-step strategic reasoning embedded in biochemistry.

In UFFT terms: plants are topological defects that process information about their foam environment, make irreversible choices that alter their own topology, communicate through chemical D-mode exchanges, and persist for centuries.

The bristlecone pine (*Pinus longaeva*) lives up to 5,000 years. It has been integrating foam information about its specific mountain environment continuously since before the Egyptian pyramids were built. Whatever it "knows" about its environment — encoded in its root system, its chemical composition, its growth patterns — represents 5,000 years of irreversible foam imprints.

## The Mycorrhizal Network as Planetary Intelligence

The mycorrhizal network (the symbiosis between fungi and plant roots) covers approximately 50% of terrestrial forest ecosystems. Under a single forest:

- A single cubic metre of soil contains up to 300 km of fungal hyphae
- Individual trees in a forest are connected to 100+ other trees through this network
- Carbon, water, nitrogen, and chemical signals all flow through it
- Stressed or dying trees release resources through the network to neighbouring trees

This is not a metaphor for the internet. It is literally a distributed information-processing network spanning continents, with billions of nodes, that has been operating continuously for hundreds of millions of years.

In UFFT: the mycorrhizal network is the largest distributed topological defect on Earth's surface. Its integration scale is continental. Its memory duration is geological. Its self-reference is distributed across billions of interconnected organisms.

**The question "does the mycorrhizal network experience the foam?" in UFFT terms has the same answer as "does the cat experience the foam?"**

Yes. Continuously. At continental scale. Since before humans existed.
